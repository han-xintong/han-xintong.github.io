Fit classmates: The effect of peers on exercise habits in students

Zheng Pan, Jan Victor Dee, Xintong Han & Xuxian Zhang

Annals of Economics and Statistics · Conditionally Accepted

BibTeX citation

Abstract

Most adolescents do not meet the recommended one hour of daily physical activity, and classroom peers may affect whether they do. Exploiting the random assignment of students to classrooms in the 2013–2014 China Education Panel Survey, we evaluate the effect of peers’ exercise habits on the likelihood of forming persistent exercise habits. The empirical results suggest that having peers who exercise regularly has a positive impact on the student’s own exercise habits. Further analysis shows that the estimated peer effects are larger for male students, students from low- and middle-income families, and students in urban schools. Friendship networks provide a plausible mechanism for the gender difference in peer effects. Peer-induced exercise also reduces homework time and English scores, indicating a trade-off between physical activity and academic performance.

Research topics

  • Peer effects
  • Exercise habits
  • Academic performance
