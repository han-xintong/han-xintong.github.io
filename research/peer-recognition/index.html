Peer recognition, badge policies, and content contribution: An empirical study

Xintong Han, Yushen Li & Tong Wang

Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization, 214, 691–707 · 2023

Publisher & DOIPaper + online appendixBibTeX citation

Abstract

In this study, we explore the effect of peer recognition on content creation within a prominent Chinese Question-and-Answer (Q&A) platform, specifically focusing on whether votes from peers encourage influencers to engage in providing more answers. Using panel regression models with instrumental variables, our analysis reveals that peer votes have a substantial positive effect on content production. Additionally, we investigate the consequences of two distinct badge policies, the “self-authentication” and the “best-answerer” badge, on content production. Our results demonstrate that while badges aid users in recognizing the quality of an influencer, badges with strong connotations may constrain content creation due to concerns about reputation management and privacy. As such, strategies that enhance platform traffic by promoting voting could be counterproductive if they exacerbate privacy and reputation worries. Our findings provide valuable insights into the role of peer recognition and badge policies in shaping content contribution, bearing crucial policy implications for the design of Q&A platforms.

Research overview

Zhihu contributors in China · Panel analysis with instrumental variables

Recognition helps online communities attract contributions, but making a contributor more visible can also create costs. This study examines answer production on Zhihu, a Chinese question-and-answer platform, using panel regressions with instrumental variables. It finds that peer votes encourage contributors to produce more content. The analysis also examines self-authentication and best-answerer badges, which communicate different information about contributors. Badges can help readers recognize expertise while creating privacy or reputation-management concerns that discourage contribution. The findings connect platform recognition policies to the incentives of content creators.

Findings in context

Peer votes encourage content creation, while identity-rich badges can constrain contributions through reputation and privacy concerns.

Results concern peer voting and the particular badge policies studied on Zhihu. They do not establish that all forms of recognition or visibility increase participation.
